Abstract

I’m a 2025 cohort undergraduate student from the Shaw College of the Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen. I’m pursuing the B.Sc. in Mathematics and Applied Mathematics in the School of Science and Engineering, specializing in the Applied Mathematics Stream.

About My Name
  • Pronunciation of 于慧泉: yú huì quán
  • 于: Yu is my surname. It comes from my father. In Chinese, the surname is written first, while the given name is written last.
  • 慧泉: Hui (慧) means wisdom and being wise. Quan (泉) means spring or water source. My family gave me this name with their wish that I would be the source of wisdom.
  • Interesting fact: Collecting the initials of “Yu Hui Quan”, one can get “YHQ”, which is the same as the initials of “Yang Hua Qing”. “Yang Hua Qing” (yǎng huà qīng, 氧化氢) is the pronunciation of hydrogen oxide, the Chemical name of water, in Chinese. Simultaneously, my surname 于 has the same pronunciation as 鱼, which means fish. There is a famous Chinese phrase, 如鱼得水, meaning "like a fish to water." My name for me is like water for a fish. I deeply love my name.
  • Hurst: Hurst is a word often used in place names to indicate a wooded hill. Pay attention to the second character, 慧, of my name. On top of the character, there is something that looks like a grove; while in the middle of the character, it is a lateralized version of the word 山 (hill). Hence, the word Hurst partially describes the appearance of the second character of my name. Also, note that “Hurst” and “Huiquan” both start with “Hu”.
